The details behind the idea

Short chapters make the story useful beyond the opening headline.

The premise · 01

A lunch break with mission control

Burrito Blaster turns a very ordinary task into an intentionally ridiculous systems demo. The storytelling is playful; the underlying sequence and decisions stay exact.

  • Start with an order and a prepared burrito
  • Verify the fictional destination before any simulated launch
  • Show both success and uncertainty honestly
The safety boundary · 02

There is no actual cannon

Every operational detail is simulated. This is a human-readable walkthrough—not a dispatch system, customer lookup, SMS sender, or physical launcher.

  • Never represent sample coordinates as a real location
  • Never imply a real notification was delivered
  • Never hide pause or stop branches
The takeaway · 03

Every path should tell the truth

Good explanations have visible conditions and honest endings. The same story can be dressed as polished product engineering, a cheerful retro game, or an ornate fantasy briefing without changing the facts.

  • Primary route: six stages, from order check to completion
  • Alternate routes: pause or stop with a visible reason
  • Presentation controls explore the example; they do not execute it
